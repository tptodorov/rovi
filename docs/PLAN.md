M5 is in progress: measured geometry is flashed and low-command forward/reverse, strafe, rotation, diagonals and automatic lease stopping are demonstrated on the floor. Todor reports no performance change noticed and accepts the existing approximate m/s and rad/s estimates for open-loop planning; the extended measurement sweep is stopped. Q11/O3 is settled as open-loop duty control: no encoders, with added sensors out of scope. * Product software: the M6 product crates, simulator and firmware are implemented ([tasks](../openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-20-add-four-wheel-api/tasks.md)); laptop conformance/CI are audited, with [scoped acceptance recorded](../openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-20-add-four-wheel-api/verification.md#acceptance-review-task-53) on 2026-10-19. Archived/synced 2026-10-20; accepted requirements live in [`openspec/specs/`](../openspec/specs/). The [laptop UDP teleoperation client](../apps/teleop/README.md), selected by Todor on 2026-10-20, is implemented and tested on the laptop against fake peers/PTYs and the product simulator; its separately authorized stand batch passed with Todor’s physical confirmation on 2026-10-20 ([evidence](../firmware/bench/2026-10-20-laptop-teleop/notes.md)). ### Software

1. **S1 spike** (done 2026-10-06, laptop only) — yes, with conditions. nano-ros's XRCE backend cross-compiles for the S3, links next to Rovi's stack, and interoperates with ROS 2 Jazzy via the micro-ROS agent. Rovi would own the S3 port (13 platform symbols; blocking UDP must be bridged to embassy-net). Footprint and on-board behaviour are untested; M8 decides nano-ros vs the direct XRCE client. See [S1 findings](research/s1-micro-ros-nano-ros-spike.md).
   * **S2 spike** (done 2026-10-06, laptop only): native Rust/embassy [zenoh-nostd](https://github.com/eclipse-zenoh/zenoh-nostd) + `rmw_zenoh`. ROS 2 ↔ car data works in both directions over UDP, and a real session links into M3 at +101 KiB flash. The car is invisible to the ROS graph until liveliness tokens are added. The project is early-stage. It is the preferred path for M8's `ros` feature. See [S2 findings](research/s2-zenoh-nostd-spike.md).
2. **M8** (board-verified 2026-10-13/14; accepted except the deferred items below) — setpoint protocol with a single owner and optional ROS 2. See [`experiments/rovi-m8-setpoint-ros/`](../experiments/rovi-m8-setpoint-ros/), [ADR-0004](adr/0004-single-owner-latest-setpoint.md), [ADR-0005](adr/0005-zenoh-nostd-ros-backend.md), [ADR-0006](adr/0006-twiststamped-cdr-setpoint.md), [ADR-0011](adr/0011-ros-auto-arm.md), [ADR-0012](adr/0012-ros-router-over-tcp.md) and [ADR-0013](adr/0013-ros-owner-is-the-node.md). Summary:
   * Setpoints are `TwistStamped`, CDR-encoded on BLE, UDP and ROS, with `mecanum_drive_controller`-compatible kinematics.
   * The latest setpoint wins; there is no queue. Each valid setpoint renews the lease.
   * The first controller to claim the car (BLE connect, UDP hello, or first ROS setpoint) owns it, and the other radio shuts down until release.
   * BLE and UDP need an explicit arm; ROS 2 arms on its own stream. Every safety stop disarms, and a STOP or out-of-limits stop needs an explicit arm on every source. Claim and reclaim windows apply.
   * The car runs an AP only. ROS 2 runs over zenoh-nostd behind a compile-time `ros` feature, over TCP to `rmw_zenohd` on a laptop on the car AP.
   * **Gating (done):** every acceptance item observed on the board. **Deferred, not gating:** a second BLE central (needs a second adapter), wider phone/range qualification (M3 remainder), and the Wi-Fi stall ([#23](https://github.com/tptodorov/rovi/issues/23), open until it recurs; [15-minute no-motion baseline](../firmware/bench/2026-10-20-wifi-soak/notes.md) on 2026-10-20: 4,385 Echo replies, zero loss; [15-minute current-TCP ROS follow-up](../firmware/bench/2026-10-20-ros-wifi-soak/notes.md): 4,363 replies, zero loss or ROS disconnect, laptop power saving off; legacy UDP-router workload not replayed).
3. **M6** (software implemented 2026-10-17; scoped acceptance recorded 2026-10-19; O1–O9 closed or deferred) — the low-level four-wheel API, archived/synced 2026-10-20: [car control API](../openspec/specs/car-control-api/spec.md), [motor control](../openspec/specs/motor-control/spec.md), [change history](../openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-20-add-four-wheel-api/proposal.md). It adds a `motor-control` layer (four normalised wheel commands, standby, limits) under M8's setpoint/ownership model. The refresh carries the measured geometry, open-loop decision, deadband/duty rule and verified wheel mapping into requirements. The reviewed contracts cover fault recovery, sequence/session handling and ROS observability. The product profile caps duty at 70%, the envelope M4 verified. Measured current (needs a current monitor) and a numeric standby bound (needs a logic analyzer) are deferred and not claimed; O9 selected the verified reliable/volatile capabilities fallback. The [verification map](../openspec/changes/archive/2026-10-20-add-four-wheel-api/verification.md) names the software checks, confirmed product board results and equipment-dependent physical deferrals. Task 5.3 is complete for that scope; the archive preserves its evidence and deferrals. Earlier unconfirmed BLE runs remain development evidence only.

4. **Teleoperation clients** (2026-10-21):
   * **Laptop keyboard:** [UDP and BLE modes](../apps/teleop/README.md) share gentle speeds and explicit arm/stop controls. [Automatic reconnect/loss beep](../openspec/changes/add-teleop-reconnect/verification.md) and [native BLE mode](../openspec/changes/add-laptop-ble-teleoperation/verification.md) pass 59 host tests, including 20 BLE tests with the installed Bleak. The original UDP stand batch passed; these new recovery/BLE checks still need separate rover acceptance. Their active changes remain unarchived.
   * **iPhone:** [Bluefy BLE web controller](../apps/iphone/README.md), live at <https://tptodorov.github.io/rovi/iphone/>, developed on Linux without native-app signing. Buttons, manual reconnect and foreground-only operation are implemented; 25 Node and 10 Chromium tests pass. P1/P3/P4 have phone/serial evidence and Todor's direction/stop confirmation; [requirements](../openspec/specs/iphone-ble-teleoperation/spec.md) are archived/synced. The STOP-while-holding issue was fixed and rechecked with motor power off.
   * **Resolved phone decisions:** Q9 selected the Bluefy web route; Q18 measured ATT MTU 247 on iPhone 11 Pro/Bluefy. Q19 is accepted for this target on a reported ~80 s/1,614-write run (max submission gap 158 ms, slowest write 17 ms) and Todor's P2 closure decision. Earlier BLE drops remain unexplained, there is no serial-captured full P2 workload or measured iOS connection interval, and VoiceOver was skipped. These limits do not imply qualification for other phones, floor operation or physical stopping bounds.
   * **Next:** complete the laptop recovery/BLE stand checks. [iPhone sensor feasibility](research/iphone-motion-control-feasibility.md) is conditional on an HTTPS sensor-only diagnostic in the actual Bluefy/iPhone: permission, finite readings, cadence, calibration and lifecycle behavior. Prefer relative tilt mapped to bounded velocity, with a held deadman and independent sensor expiry. The existing BLE stream appears reusable without firmware changes. Product implementation needs a separate input-spec change; blended axes are outside the accepted button-only contract. No diagnostic, sensor implementation or new rover test has run.

### Hardware

* **M8 and M3 remainder** (deferred, see M8 above); M4 and basic M5 work have since run on the assembled car.
* **M4** (complete, 2026-10-17) — four motors on the M8 firmware, wheels off the ground, minimal wiring, a software fail-safe ([ADR-0014](adr/0014-m4-minimal-wiring-software-fail-safe.md)) and a soldered carrier ([build guide](../experiments/rovi-m4-four-motor-safety/build/README.md)). Mapping and direction confirmed; start threshold 35 %, so the motor layer runs 40-100 % duty; no reset on reversal; every stop trigger stops all four; 5 min at 70 % stays cool. Open, not gating: motor capacitors, the motor power bank's auto-sleep (manually woken for supervised floor tests; unattended power policy remains open). See [`experiments/rovi-m4-four-motor-safety/`](../experiments/rovi-m4-four-motor-safety/).
* **M5** (basic characterization recorded, 2026-10-17) — low-command floor motion and stops demonstrated using M4's setup; existing approximate motion references accepted by Todor, no performance change noticed, extended testing stopped. Accuracy and controlled battery/load effects remain unverified. Motors have no encoders; adding them is out of scope. See [`experiments/rovi-m5-mecanum-motion/`](../experiments/rovi-m5-mecanum-motion/).

### Done

* **M1** (complete, bench-tested 2026-10-02) — prove one ESP32-S3 + one TB6612FNG channel drives one motor, on real hardware. See [`experiments/rovi-m1-tb6612/MILESTONE.md`](../experiments/rovi-m1-tb6612/MILESTONE.md).
* **M2** (complete, bench-tested 2026-10-02) — ESP32-S3 as a BLE peripheral: advertises a custom GATT service, a generic BLE client writes drive commands, firmware reacts observably (LED/serial). Bare board, no motor hardware — independent of M1. See [`experiments/rovi-m2-ble/`](../experiments/rovi-m2-ble/).
* **M3** (hardware-validated 2026-10-04; 5 of 6 acceptance items) — direct WPA2 car AP and shared BLE/Wi-Fi command ingress, board-only. Shared ingress, queue/stale/stop, watchdog-under-backlog and per-transport loss pass on the board. A 2 s TCP socket timeout caps Wi-Fi silence below the configured watchdog. M8 supersedes its TCP+FIFO design. See [`experiments/rovi-m3-wifi-queue/`](../experiments/rovi-m3-wifi-queue/).

## Deferred decisions (non-goals for now)

Considered and deliberately set aside — revisit only if the premise changes:

* **Autonomy** is not a committed near-term goal. The low-level API shouldn't preclude it, but nothing is architected around it yet.
* **Splitting the platform into its own repo** is deferred until a second real hardware application (boat, drone, etc.) needs to consume it independently of this car.
* **Video teleoperation, cameras and onboard Linux compute.** M7 was dropped on 2026-10-06 (not a priority), and its experiment folder was removed. The ESP32-S3 stays the car's only network endpoint. Recover it from git history if video returns.
* **An RTOS or ESP-IDF** is not used; firmware is bare-metal Rust on Embassy ([ADR-0007](adr/0007-rust-embassy-no-rtos.md)).
